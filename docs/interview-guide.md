# Critical-incident interview guide

The goal is not to ask an experienced scheduler to enumerate “all rules.” It is to recover decisions from concrete incidents, especially rules that only become visible after a draft exists.

## Opening

- Tell me about the first timetable you produced and the first complaint it triggered.
- Which facts were written down, and which did you only learn after someone objected?
- When you now open a draft, what do you inspect first?

## Walk through a real incident

- What exact cell did you move, and what made it unacceptable?
- Was the problem physically impossible, against an explicit school rule, a tacit convention, a policy preference, or one person’s preference?
- Who had authority to relax it?
- What else changed because of that move?
- Could a local swap have repaired it, or did the whole schedule need reconsideration?

## Preference elicitation

- Which teachers have explicitly stated preferences?
- Which preferences are inferred from past complaints?
- Who is genuinely indifferent and therefore provides useful flexibility?
- Which preferences conflict, and how do you decide whose cost is larger?

## Explainability and acceptance

- When the schedule is impossible, what explanation would let you act?
- Which relaxation levers are legitimate for you to test?
- How long do teachers need to inspect Draft 1, and what makes a region “accepted/locked”?
- What evidence must be retained so the next scheduler does not have to store the rules in anxiety and memory?

## Capture format after each incident

Record: observed action, reason in the scheduler’s words, proposed category, affected entities, authority to relax, confidence, and a minimal regression fixture. Do not promote an inferred preference into a school-wide rule without confirmation.
